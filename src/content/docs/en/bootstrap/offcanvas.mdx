An offcanvas keeps secondary content out of the way until the user asks for it: a course index, a filter panel, a cart. It covers part of the page and has a backdrop.

## When to use it

<div class="ds-do-dont">
<Rule kind="do" title="Use it">

- Use it for secondary content or navigation on small screens.
- Give it a title and a close button.
- Start on the left for navigation and on the right for details.

</Rule>
<Rule kind="dont" title="Don't">

- Use it for a decision; use a modal.
- Open it on its own.
- Put the main content of the page in it.

</Rule>
</div>

## Anatomy

A panel (`offcanvas`, with a side) with a **header** (`offcanvas-header`, `offcanvas-title`, close) and a **body** (`offcanvas-body`), over a dimmed page.

## Combinations

| Combination | Use it for |
|---|---|
| Start × navigation | From the left for a course index or a menu. |
| End × details | From the right for filters or the details of a row. |
| Offcanvas × small screens | The panel that is a sidebar on a desktop becomes an offcanvas on a phone. |

## What Didactika changes and why

| What we changed | Why it helps |
|---|---|
| The panel is a surface with a soft deep shadow | It floats over the page and does not look glued to the edge. |
| The list inside takes the same states as every list | The user already knows how the items answer. |

## Classes

| Class | What it does |
|---|---|
| `offcanvas`, `offcanvas-start`, `offcanvas-end` | The panel and its side |
| `offcanvas-header`, `offcanvas-title`, `offcanvas-body` | Its parts |
| `btn-close` | Close button |

## Accessibility

- Focus moves into the panel and returns to the trigger when it closes.
- Escape closes it.
- `aria-labelledby` points at the title.
