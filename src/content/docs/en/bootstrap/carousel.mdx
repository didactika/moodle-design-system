A carousel shows a few related slides in the same space. Use it sparingly: people often miss what is not on the first slide.

## When to use it

<div class="ds-do-dont">
<Rule kind="do" title="Use it">

- Three to five slides, the most important first.
- Show the controls and the position indicators.
- Do not start it automatically.

</Rule>
<Rule kind="dont" title="Don't">

- Hide essential content in later slides.
- Auto-advance faster than people can read.
- Put long text or forms in slides.

</Rule>
</div>

## Anatomy

A frame (`carousel`) with **slides** (`carousel-item`, one `active`), optional **captions**, **previous and next** controls and **indicators**.

## Combinations

| Combination | Use it for |
|---|---|
| Controls × indicators | Both: arrows to move, dots to know where you are and jump. |
| Slides × captions | A short caption over the slide; the text must read on the image. |
| Carousel × autoplay | Off. The user moves it. |

## What Didactika changes and why

| What we changed | Why it helps |
|---|---|
| The controls are round buttons with a dark tint | They are visible over any image and are as big as a button, so they are easy to hit. |
| The current indicator is longer than the rest | Position is read from the shape and not from the colour of a dot. |
| The frame is rounded and clips the slides | It matches the cards around it. |

## Classes

| Class | What it does |
|---|---|
| `carousel`, `slide` | The frame |

| `carousel-indicators` | Position dots |
| `carousel-caption` | Text over a slide |

## Accessibility

- Controls are buttons or links with text for assistive technology.
- Let the user pause or stop movement; do not auto-play.
- Each slide has text alternatives.
