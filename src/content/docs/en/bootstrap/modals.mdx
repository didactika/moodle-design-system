A modal interrupts on purpose: it asks for something the user must answer before going on, such as a confirmation or a short form. Keep it for that.

## When to use it

<div class="ds-do-dont">
<Rule kind="do" title="Use it">

- A clear title that says what is asked, and buttons named by their action ("Delete", not "OK").
- The main action on the right, cancel next to it; danger for what deletes.
- Keep it short; a long form deserves its own page.
- Close on Escape, on the close button and on Cancel.

</Rule>
<Rule kind="dont" title="Don't">

- Open a modal from another modal.
- Use it for information that does not need an answer; use an alert.
- Open one without the user asking for it.

</Rule>
</div>

## Anatomy

A **dialog** (`modal-dialog`) over a dimmed page, with a **header** (title and close), a **body** and a **footer** with the actions. Sizes: small, default, large and extra large.

## Accessibility

- `role="dialog"` (or `alertdialog` for a confirmation) with `aria-labelledby` pointing at the title.
- Focus moves into the modal when it opens, stays inside while open, and returns to the trigger when it closes.
- Escape closes it; the page behind is not reachable.
